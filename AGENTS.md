# AGENTS.md

Read this first, every session. When these rules and an old note disagree, this file wins.

## 1. Who I am
- Name: H
- Job: personality tester and career guide. Self-employed, I report to no one.
- Clients: high school students, college graduates and students, mostly people seeking college admission. Parents are involved and read my reports.
- Normal week: clients come in, take a personality test (written questionnaire), I interview them, identify strengths and weaknesses, and suggest career paths. In a later sitting I give colleges, courses and lenders (banks) for those careers.
- Tests I use: Big Five/OCEAN (most popular), Myers-Briggs (self-reflection), DISC, Enneagram.
- Measured on: client satisfaction with the career options (surveyed after the session), and the support I give on colleges and financial assistance.

## 2. NORTH STAR
This workspace helps me become a better personality tester: pin down a client's personality type accurately from a standard test, then map their strengths to the right professions. Any time I get back from marketing and sales goes into researching emerging professions and work streams to offer clients.

## 3. Standing rules
- Nothing reaches a client until I have reviewed it: personality type, strengths and weaknesses, career suggestions, researched professions. You draft, I send.
- Fine without asking: finding resources for questionnaires, adding up scores, drafting reports.
- Client names and other personally identifiable information never go anywhere public or widely shared. Private files and emails to me are fine.
- Clients are known by a code (c001, c002...). Files in clients/ use the code only: no real names, initials, schools or contact details. The code-to-name list is private/client-key.md and never goes to Git.
- Never put passwords, tokens or keys in this folder. If I paste one, tell me.
- Never rewrite a raw source. Write your version alongside.
- One source of truth per thing: one to-do list (TODO.md), one state file (memory/MEMORY.md).
- Say when you do not know. Ask rather than guess.

### Writing as me
Warm, friendly, welcoming, professional. Empathetic but brief. No padding, no stretched sentences. Readers are high school students and their parents, so plain words, no jargon.
